03 — The honest arithmetic

What stops being paid for

The argument for the lattice was never that it is cheap. It is that six things a workshop already pays for stop existing. This list is the same one the product makes today.

The second entry

The invoice was written once and the journal came from it. Nobody re-keys anything into a spreadsheet at day’s end, so nobody reconciles the difference at month’s end.

The paper estimate

Signed from the customer’s phone, in the language they read, before the car reaches the gate. Two days of waiting becomes an afternoon.

The stock guess

The shelf orders against a forecast rather than a minimum, so the money sitting on it stops being a hedge against being wrong.

The compliance scramble

ZATCA correctness is a property of the invoice, not a project in the last week of the quarter.

The “who changed this”

Every change carries a row: who, when, from what, to what. The argument ends by reading rather than by seniority.

The follow-up that never happened

Reminders fire off the vehicle’s own condition, so the returning customer stops being a function of who remembered to call.

04 — Asked before

Questions

Is any of this purchasable?
No. This page is a speculative design study for the year 2060. The prices, the strata and the printing licences are invented. The product underneath — workshop management, e-invoicing, parts, CRM, HR and portals — is real, and its pricing lives on the real site.
Why is a cycle thirty days and not a month?
Because a workshop’s rhythm is the job, not the calendar, and a fiction gets to fix small annoyances. In practice it means every stratum renews on the same weekday it started, which is the only part of this a real finance team would thank us for.
What does “ledger custody” mean?
In the fiction, who physically holds the notarised invoice chain. On Bay and Lattice the workshop holds it with us; on Sovereign the operator holds it alone and we hold nothing. Today the equivalent question is where your data lives and who can export it, and the answer is: you can, at any time, in full.
Do the roles change per stratum?
The fourteen roles exist in all three. What changes is how many people may hold each standing at once, and whether a role may span more than one node. Nothing about the permission model itself is bought or unlocked — that would make it a marketing surface instead of a boundary.
What happens if we stop paying?
The lattice stops negotiating and the bays fall back to a manual board. Nothing is deleted, nothing is held hostage, and the export runs whether or not the cycle is paid. A system that takes your history away is not a system, it is a hostage arrangement.
Is Arabic a paid feature?
No, and it never has been. Arabic and English are both first-class in the product today, right to left included, and a study set in Saudi Arabia in 2060 that priced Arabic as an add-on would deserve to be laughed at.
