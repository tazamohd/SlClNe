02 — How a part reaches the bay

Four steps, no lorry from abroad

The same four steps a storekeeper does today, with the waiting taken out of three of them.

P/01

Identify

The part is named off the VIN rather than off a memory, so the wrong one does not arrive two days later. Still a purchase order, still needing an approval before it spends.

P/02

Find

Eleven branches are checked before any supplier is. Most “we have to order that” parts are already in the group — ninety minutes away, not eleven days.

P/03

Receive

Booked in against the job, not just into stock, so the car it is for is the thing that moves. Someone signs for it — that has not changed and should not.

P/04

Fit and cost

It leaves stock as it is fitted, and the invoice has it costed before the car is off the lift. One write, one journal, no re-keying.

What is not built

Live supplier feeds, group stock, vans

Group-wide stock visibility needs every branch on one tenancy and counting honestly — a discipline problem more than a software one. Live supplier availability needs each supplier to expose it, which is a commercial negotiation, not a sprint. The vans need vans.

  • Live availability and pricing from suppliers
  • One stock view across eleven branches
  • Nine vans, staffed and scheduled
