01 — The shape of it

Subsystem constellation

Nothing here is a layer stacked on another. Every subsystem talks to the middle and to its neighbours directly, which is why an invoice can write a journal, a journal can flag a part, and a part landing late can move a car’s slot — without a person carrying the message between four systems. 03 — Who it answers to

Fourteen roles

A role is not a menu; it is a boundary. Every screen, every field and every write is checked against the standing of whoever asked, and the refusal is logged as carefully as the change. This is the one part of the page with no 2030 column: it works this way now.

04 — What holds it up

The spine

Four things carry everything above. Remove any one and this is a filing cabinet with a login.

S/01

The floor

Bays, lifts, tools, vans and the cars themselves. Everything that touches metal — reporting as it happens rather than when somebody remembers to write it down.

S/02

The board

Where the day is decided. It is the bay board that ships today, with enough context to propose the next move — and a human who still approves it.

S/03

The core

Reads the port and the history and hands the technician what fails next with a confidence attached, instead of a fault code and a page number.

S/04

The ledger

Every movement of money, parts or authority — written once, hashed at issue, and kept by the customer for the life of the car.

The hard part was never the diagnosis. It was that six people wrote the same number into four systems and one of them was wrong by Thursday.
